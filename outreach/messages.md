# Outreach — Gulf real estate agencies

Personalised, not mass-sent. Every line marked `[FILL]` must be filled with
something real you looked up on their website or listing page. A message with a
placeholder left in it burns the agency's name forever.

Volume target: **5 personalised messages per day, every weekday.** That is the
single highest-leverage activity in this business. Code is 30% of the work;
this is the other 70%.

---

## Rules that protect the account

1. **One message, then wait 5–7 days.** No follow-up storms. Replying twice to a
   non-reply is how you get blocked.
2. **No link in message 1.** Ask, don't pitch. The link goes in message 2 if
   they engage at all. Cold DMs with links get filtered.
3. **Never claim a result you have not produced.** You have no case studies yet.
   Say "I've built X" and offer the free discovery call. Do not invent numbers.
4. **English first for UAE/Kuwait/Qatar; Arabic is welcome but do not assume.**
   Gulf agency owners are often more comfortable in English.
5. **Stop immediately on any negative reply.** One "not interested" ends it.

## Message 1 — the observation

The opener is a specific thing you noticed, not a compliment. "Love your work"
is indistinguishable from spam.

**EN**

> Hi [FILL: first name],
>
> I was looking at your [FILL: specific listing/project — name it] and noticed
> it's been up [FILL: estimate] — the enquiry form on the page doesn't show a
> WhatsApp option or a callback, so anyone browsing at 10pm has nowhere to go
> except email.
>
> For a [FILL: area] agency, that's usually the most expensive gap you can have:
> the leads are there, they just arrive when the office is shut.

> I build small systems that reply in under 5 minutes, day or night. Not a
> platform, not an annual contract — just a reply and a follow-up.
>
> Would it be useful if I sent you a working example you can run yourself? No
> cost, and you can judge it before we ever speak.

## Discovery call — the 6 questions

You are qualifying, not selling. If the answer to Q6 is bad, say so and walk
away. Chasing a bad fit costs more than it earns and burns the relationship.

1. How many enquiries do you get in a typical month?
2. What's your current median time from enquiry to first contact? *(Ask them to
   check, not guess. If they don't know, that is itself a finding.)*
3. What percentage of those enquiries become a viewing? And of those, a deal?
4. What happens to an enquiry that arrives after 6pm?
5. How many people are manually re-contacting leads, and how long does that
   take them weekly?
6. **If I showed you this working, what would it be worth per month?** — *Do not
   answer this yourself. Silence is fine. First price spoken is the one that
   anchors the deal.*

Write their exact figures down. The proposal generator needs them, and a
proposal built on their own numbers is what closes against a competitor quoting
generic benchmarks.

---

## Price anchoring

- Present **three** tiers. Most people take the middle one.
- Quote the **setup fee and the retainer separately** — never one lump sum.
- If they push on price, reduce **scope** (fewer workflows), not price. A
  discounted retainer is the first thing cancelled.
- **Price for the Gulf, not for Egypt.** A project a Cairo client budgets at
  $2,000 is routinely $5,000–8,000 from a Gulf client. Quoting regional
  lowball rates is leaving real money on the table.
- Take **30–50% upfront** on fixed-price work.

---

## Kill criteria — walk away if

- They cannot give you leads-per-month or response time. Without both, the
  proposal is guesswork and you will get blamed for a number you invented.
- They want a custom platform, not automation of what they have.
- Price is the entire conversation.
- They expect the system to replace staff rather than remove a task.
